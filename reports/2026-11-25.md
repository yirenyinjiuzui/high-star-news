## 1. calibre

Give a folder of ebooks a searchable catalog.

### What it does

Import books, edit title and author metadata, view them locally, and convert supported formats for a reading device. The catalog keeps multiple formats of a title together.

### Why we recommend it

Start here when inconsistent author names or filenames make a collection hard to search.

### How it could help you

1. Give the volumes of a series a common series name and a reading order.
2. Keep an EPUB original alongside a converted copy for a second device.

### Who it fits and what to expect

Free GPL desktop application for Windows, macOS and Linux. Allow disk space for the library and converted copies. Conversion of a fixed-layout PDF may scramble columns or footnotes. A single book that already opens well needs little catalog work.

### A small first step

Set aside 20 minutes. Import two public-domain EPUBs into a small library, standardize the author spelling and add a tag. Search by that tag, then open each result to check that its title matches the cover.

## 2. KOReader

Make a dense page easier to read on a small screen.

### What it does

KOReader adjusts margins, line spacing and contrast. It also offers zoom for fixed pages and reflow for PDF or DjVu scans. Its paginated menus are designed for e-ink screens.

### Why we recommend it

It is worth trying when wide margins or small fixed text make you pan across every line.

### How it could help you

1. Adjust spacing in an EPUB until a paragraph is comfortable to follow on your device.
2. Compare zoom and reflow on a two-column public-domain scan and check the reading order.

### Who it fits and what to expect

Free AGPL software with builds for Android, Linux and selected e-readers. Installation depends on the exact model and firmware. Tables, illustrations and columns can lose context during reflow. Android is a convenient trial platform if the e-reader needs extra setup.

### A small first step

Allow 15–20 minutes on a supported device with the reader ready. Open one scanned page, fit its content to the width, then turn reflow on. Read a paragraph across a column break and choose the mode that preserves the intended order.

## 3. Koodo Reader

Take highlighted passages and your own comments out of the reader.

### What it does

Koodo imports EPUB, PDF and other formats, and supports bookmarks, highlighting and notes. Export options include Markdown and CSV, which let you work with excerpts in a text editor or spreadsheet.

### Why we recommend it

Choose it when the deliverable is a reading log you can keep working on after closing the book.

### How it could help you

1. Collect passages from one chapter in Markdown for a discussion outline.
2. Open exported notes in a spreadsheet and group comments by theme.

### Who it fits and what to expect

Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS and web versions are available. Basic reading has a free tier. The official plan comparison lists a 20-book free mobile limit and cross-device library sync under Pro. Keep space for imports and exports. Check the plan if you intend to carry a large mobile library. See https://www.koodoreader.com/en/pricing .

### A small first step

Allow 15 minutes. Import a sample EPUB, highlight two passages and add your own question to one. Export the notes as Markdown and check whether you can distinguish each excerpt from your comment without reopening the book.

## 4. Readest

Read a passage beside its translation or commentary.

### What it does

Parallel Read displays two documents in separate panes. Search, bookmarks and highlights help locate corresponding passages even when editions use different pagination.

### Why we recommend it

It suits a comparison in which you frequently return to the exact wording of a second text.

### How it could help you

1. Compare two public-domain translations of the same paragraph.
2. Keep a commentary open beside the primary text and mark a point of disagreement.

### Who it fits and what to expect

The AGPL reader runs on desktop, mobile and the web. Two panes benefit from a wide screen. Local reading is free. The free plan lists 500 MB of cloud sync storage, with larger quotas on paid plans. See https://readest.com/pricing .

### A small first step

Allow 20 minutes on a laptop. Open two editions side by side, find the same paragraph by its opening words and highlight one difference in each. Check whether both texts remain readable at your preferred font size.

## 5. Calibre-Web

Browse an existing calibre library from a second device.

### What it does

Calibre-Web reads a valid calibre database and exposes a searchable catalog, browser reader and OPDS feed. Per-user permissions control actions such as downloading a book.

### Why we recommend it

Consider it when several readers need access to the same collection and someone can maintain the host.

### How it could help you

1. Browse a household library on a tablet while its files stay on the home server.
2. Let an OPDS-compatible reading app find a title from a shared catalog.

### Who it fits and what to expect

GPL-licensed web application. It needs a running host with access to the book files and metadata.db, plus a browser or compatible client. Budget for storage, backups and uptime. Optional format conversion needs calibre binaries. A single offline reader usually has little use for this setup.

### A small first step

Allow 25–30 minutes with an available host and a two-book test library. Follow the local quick start, point it at the test library and change the initial administrator password. Create a reader account and verify which of the two titles it can browse and download.
